What to eat tonight?

CP
Chef Parth
30 September 2025 · 4 min read

Eating healthy often comes with a lot of myths that make it tough to navigate nutrition. You may have heard claims like "carbs are bad" or "eating healthy costs too much." As someone passionate about healthy eating, I want to clear up these misconceptions. In this post, I'm unveiling common myths about healthy eating and offering you five practical, science-backed tips that cater to your unique needs.

Let's explore the world of nutrition and discover how to make smart choices that enhance our health while still enjoying the pleasure of eating.

Myth 1: Carbs are the enemy

A popular belief in dieting is that carbohydrates are bad for our health. Many think that cutting carbs is essential for weight loss and better health. However, this idea is misleading.

The science behind carbs

Carbohydrates are our main source of energy. They are crucial for brain function and physical activity. Not all carbs are created equal; whole grains, fruits, and vegetables offer fiber, vitamins, and minerals. A study showed that consuming whole grains can reduce the risk of heart disease by 25%.

Tip 1: Choose whole grains

Instead of eliminating carbs, choose whole grains like brown rice, barley, and quinoa. These options provide lasting energy and help you feel fuller longer. Studies show that replacing refined grains with whole grains can improve weight management by promoting satiety.

Myth 2: All fats are bad

Another widespread misconception is that all fats lead to weight gain and should be avoided. In truth, fats are essential for our bodies.

The science behind fats

Healthy fats from sources like avocados, nuts, and olive oil have a vital role in hormone production and nutrient absorption. For instance, the Mediterranean diet, rich in healthy fats, has been shown to lower the risk of chronic diseases by about 30%.

Tip 2: Incorporate healthy fats

Add healthy fats to your meals. For example, sprinkle a handful of walnuts onto your salad or drizzle extra virgin olive oil on cooked vegetables. These choices boost flavor and contribute to your overall health.

Myth 3: You must count calories to lose weight

Many people believe strict calorie counting is the only path to weight loss. While knowing your calorie intake is crucial, it's not the only factor for successful weight management.

The science behind calories

Losing weight is more complex than simply eating fewer calories. The quality of the food we consume, our metabolism, and our activity levels matter greatly. Research shows that people who focus on the quality of their food rather than just calories tend to sustain weight loss better and are generally healthier.

Tip 3: Focus on nutrient density

Instead of fixating on calories, aim for nutrient-dense foods that provide essential vitamins and minerals. Filling your plate with colorful fruits like strawberries (which have a high vitamin C content) and leafy greens can energize your diet effectively.

Myth 4: Eating healthy is expensive

A common fear about healthy eating is that it's too costly. While some healthy foods can be pricey, many budget-friendly options exist.

The science behind budget-friendly eating

Eating healthy on a budget is entirely possible. Seasonal fruits and vegetables, whole grains, and legumes are typically affordable and versatile for various meals. At Friska Wellness, we pick the best ingredients from the farmers' markets, getting the best price and quality in bulk.

Tip 4: Plan your meals

Take time to plan your weekly meals. This will help you stick to your nutrition goals and save money. Look for sales and buy in bulk to make your grocery bill more manageable. For example, stock up on dried beans, which can be cooked in multiple dishes throughout the week.

Myth 5: You have to follow a strict diet

The belief that you must stick to a rigid diet to be healthy is another myth that can frustrate many. In reality, maintaining flexibility is crucial for sustainable healthy eating.

The science behind flexibility

Strict diets can lead to feelings of deprivation and may be hard to maintain long-term. A flexible eating approach allows for a variety of foods while still making healthy choices. This flexibility can lead to a lot more satisfaction, making it easier to stick to healthy eating. At Friska, we do not repeat any dishes for 2–4 months.

Tip 5: Customize your nutrition

Pay attention to your body and customize your nutrition based on what feels right for you. Incorporate foods you enjoy and give yourself permission to indulge occasionally. Balance is the foundation of healthy eating.

Embracing wholesome nutrition

Navigating healthy eating doesn't have to feel overwhelming. By debunking myths and emphasizing balanced nutrition, we can make smart choices that support our well-being. Remember, it's not about cutting out certain foods; it's about embracing a variety of nutrient-rich options that cater to your needs.

At Friska, we focus on wholesome nutrition that goes beyond strict calorie counting and emphasizes balance. We believe you can enjoy your meals while nourishing your body.

So, when you ask yourself, "What to eat tonight?" think about how you can embrace the joy of food while making choices that enhance your health without sacrificing taste or satisfaction. Happy eating.
